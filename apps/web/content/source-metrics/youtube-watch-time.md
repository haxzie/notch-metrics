## Minutes, reported as hours

The YouTube Analytics API returns `estimatedMinutesWatched`. Notch Metrics
holds the value in seconds, as every duration in the product is, and shows it
in hours because a channel of any size produces a minute figure too long to
read at a glance in the notch.

## Why it beats views

Views count arrivals. Watch time counts attention, and it is roughly what
decides whether YouTube shows a video to anybody else. A video with fewer
views and far more watch time is the one working.

Watch time rises when you publish more, when videos get longer, and when
people stay. Those are three different things, so pair this with average view
duration: if watch time climbs while average view duration falls, you are
getting reach rather than retention.

Buckets are Pacific Time, like every YouTube figure.
